# Situation Appraisal — Vessel & Co

Three versions: direct, narrative, and proposal-ready.

---

## Version 1: Direct

### Current Situation

Vessel & Co is a yacht operations company based in the Bahamas and Florida. They manage vessels for private owners and charter operators — handling maintenance scheduling, charter bookings, broker coordination, crew management, expense tracking, compliance documentation, provisioning, and dockage logistics. Their team comes from the water: captains, chefs, stews who built the business around a simple premise — put owners first, handle the operational complexity so they don't have to.

The service offering is real and operational. The systems behind it are not. Vessel & Co coordinates across owners, brokers, crew, guests, and vendors using spreadsheets. Owners maintain their own. Vessel & Co maintains a separate set. Charter calendars, maintenance logs, expense reports, crew documentation, insurance reminders — all tracked manually across disconnected files. As the number of vessels and clients grows, this approach doesn't scale. Updates fall through cracks. Information lives in too many places.

The team built a prototype mobile app to define what a centralized system should look like. It maps out role-based views for owners, managers, and crew. It models the data — vessels, tasks, documents, expenses, calendar events. But it's a visual demo running on hardcoded data with no backend. It shows the vision. It doesn't run the business.

### Desired Situation

One shared system. Owners open the app and see their vessels — upcoming charters, maintenance status, expenses, documents. Managers assign work orders, coordinate with vendors, track costs against estimates, and keep compliance documents current. Crew see what's due, mark work complete, log hours. Charter calendars sync with broker communications. Expense tracking produces post-charter reports instead of someone assembling them from receipts. Crew bios, contracts, and certifications live in one place instead of scattered files.

The app is in the App Store — iOS and Android. Vessel & Co administers it: onboarding new owners, linking vessels to organizations, managing user access. The spreadsheets are retired. The system matches the professionalism Vessel & Co already delivers in person.

### Why They Reached Out

Vessel & Co has a running business, a clear product vision, and a prototype that documents exactly what they need. They aren't looking for someone to define the product or design the experience — those decisions are made. The gap is technical execution. The prototype needs to become a real application with authentication, a database, file storage, and an App Store presence. Vessel & Co needs a developer who can assess what exists, decide what's reusable, build the backend, and ship — on a tight timeline. They're last-mile, not early-stage.

## Version 2: Narrative

### Current Situation

Vessel & Co manages yachts. Not in the abstract — they handle the daily operational weight that comes with owning or chartering a vessel. Maintenance scheduling, dockage, repairs, insurance renewals, crew sourcing and training, charter bookings, broker communication, guest provisioning, expense tracking, compliance paperwork. They operate out of the Bahamas and Florida, and their team knows the work firsthand. They've been captains, chefs, and stews. Their whole model is built on a straightforward idea: owners shouldn't have to manage the complexity of their own asset.

The business works. The infrastructure behind it doesn't match. Right now, Vessel & Co and their clients coordinate through spreadsheets. An owner has a spreadsheet tracking their vessel's maintenance and expenses. Vessel & Co has another one tracking the same vessel from their side, plus every other vessel they manage. Charter calendars, crew certifications, insurance deadlines, provisioning requests, work orders — all living in separate files, updated manually, shared over email or text. It works when you're managing a few boats. It breaks as you grow.

They saw this coming. The team built a prototype app — a React Native project that maps the entire product: role-based dashboards for owners, managers, and crew; screens for tasks, documents, calendar, supplies, analytics. It's detailed enough to show every data type and every user flow. But it runs on fake data. There's no database, no login system, no file storage. It's a blueprint that happens to look like an app.

### Desired Situation

The spreadsheets go away. Vessel & Co becomes the platform, not just the service. An owner opens the app after a charter and sees the expense report already assembled — receipts uploaded, costs categorized, ready to review. A manager checks the maintenance calendar and sees what's overdue across the fleet, assigns a work order, attaches the vendor quote. A crew member finishes an oil change, logs engine hours, marks the task complete. Insurance is expiring in 30 days — the system flags it before anyone has to remember.

Charter calendars show holds and confirmations in one view. Crew bios, contracts, and certifications are accessible to anyone who needs them. Documents don't live on someone's laptop. The app is in the App Store, running on real data, and Vessel & Co manages it themselves — onboarding clients, linking vessels, controlling access. The system matches what the business already is: clean, organized, professional.

### Why They Reached Out

Vessel & Co doesn't need someone to tell them what to build. The prototype is the clearest product brief a developer could ask for — every screen is a decision already made, every data type is a requirement already defined. What they need is someone who builds production software. Someone who can look at a prototype full of mock data and know exactly what backend to put behind it, how to handle auth, where to store documents, and how to get it through App Store review. The timeline is tight, the vision is locked, and the work is execution. They need a builder who ships.

## Version 3: Proposal-Ready

### Current Situation

Vessel & Co is a yacht operations management company based in the Bahamas and Florida, serving private owners, charter operators, and owner-operators. Their service scope covers the full operational lifecycle of yacht ownership: preventative maintenance and repairs, charter scheduling and broker coordination, crew sourcing and training, compliance and documentation management, expense tracking, provisioning, dockage logistics, and guest experience management. The team brings direct industry experience — they have worked as captains, chefs, and crew — and the company's model emphasizes owner control, transparent pricing, and operational clarity.

Vessel & Co currently coordinates these operations through manual systems. Owners and boat managers maintain their own spreadsheets for vessel data, maintenance records, and expenses. Vessel & Co's team maintains a separate parallel system tracking the same information from the management side, along with data for every other vessel under contract. Charter calendars, crew documentation, compliance deadlines, provisioning requests, and work orders are tracked across disconnected files and communicated informally. This approach creates duplication, limits visibility between parties, and does not scale with fleet growth.

The Vessel & Co team invested in a prototype mobile application to define the product requirements. Built in React Native, the prototype includes role-based interfaces for owners, managers, and crew, with screens for task management, document storage, calendar scheduling, supply tracking, expense monitoring, and fleet analytics. The prototype successfully models the data architecture, user flows, and access patterns that reflect Vessel & Co's operational structure. However, it runs entirely on mock data — no authentication, no database, no file storage, no API integration. It serves as a functional specification, not a working product.

### Desired Situation

Vessel & Co requires a production mobile application — iOS and Android — that centralizes yacht operations into a single shared platform. Owners access real-time vessel status, charter schedules, maintenance history, and expense reporting. Managers create and assign work orders, coordinate vendor activity, track estimated versus actual costs, and maintain compliance documentation. Crew receive task assignments, log completed work, and update maintenance records. Charter calendars reflect current booking status — holds, confirmations, and guest details. Document management covers insurance policies, registration, certifications, and crew contracts with expiry tracking and category organization.

The application supports Vessel & Co's existing service model: multiple owners, multiple vessels, role-based access, and flexible organizational structures. Vessel & Co administers the platform — onboarding new clients, linking vessels to organizations, and managing user permissions — without ongoing developer involvement. The system reflects the operational standard Vessel & Co already delivers: clean, professional, and owner-first.

### Why They Reached Out

Vessel & Co has completed the product definition phase. The existing prototype demonstrates a thorough understanding of data requirements, user roles, and operational workflows specific to yacht management. The company is not seeking product strategy, UX design, or requirements gathering — those decisions are documented in the prototype. The engagement is technical execution: architecting a backend infrastructure, implementing real authentication with organization-level access control, building persistent data storage, integrating document management, preparing App Store submissions for both platforms, and delivering a stable, maintainable application. Vessel & Co requires a developer who can assess the existing work, identify reusable components, make sound architectural decisions, and deliver a production application within a constrained timeline.
